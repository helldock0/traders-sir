# Retail dip-buying fragility: short a yen cross at the Tokyo open after a five-day decline of at least 1 x ATR20 during which the TFX Click 365 retail open interest grew by 10 or 15 percent or more, against a light-accumulation control (open interest up at most 2 percent on the same decline), holding 2 or 4 weekdays  (`jpy_retail_dipbuy_fragility`)

Family: retail_positioning_fragility_jpy | verdict: **kill:stage_a** | failing gates: GA_stage_a_floor | 2.6s

## Rationale
Mechanism: latent forced supply from an underwater leveraged retail book. Who trades: Japanese retail margin traders, of whom TFX Click 365 is the exchange-traded slice (350,000 to 600,000 open USDJPY contracts in the design years; the OTC margin book is about ten times larger and behaves the same way). They are structurally long the yen crosses for carry and contrarian: they add longs as the crosses fall. Descriptive, design years only: AUDJPY daily open-interest changes correlate -0.26 with daily returns, open interest rises about 3.5 percent on down days and falls 2.2 percent on up days; on five-day declines of at least 1 x ATR20 the five-day open-interest growth has quartiles +4, +9 and +18 percent, so the retail book grows into almost every decline and the question is how much. Why it matters: the positions are 10 to 25 times levered with broker-enforced losscuts, so a book that grew heavily into a decline is latent forced supply: if the decline extends, losscuts fire in a cascade and the move accelerates (the BIS Quarterly Review of December 2019 attributes the 3 January 2019 yen flash crash in large part to Japanese retail losscuts; the August 2015 and January-February 2016 yen-cross drops showed the same pattern), so after heavy accumulation into weakness the return distribution of the cross over the next days is negatively skewed and a short has a positive expectation; after a decline that retail did not buy (the light arm, including days the book shrank, where the losscuts have already happened), there is no latent supply and no continuation is expected, or a bounce. Who is on the other side: real money and CTAs selling the carry, whose selling retail absorbs. The 2011 Bank of Japan Review found that retail margin traders dampen volatility in calm times, which says the expected edge is small outside stress and is why this test pre-registers a one-sided direction (fade the retail accumulation), a monotonicity check (15 percent >= 10 percent >= light) and a modest expectation. What has been measured before: cross_zfade found two-sigma moves on crosses keep running at the daily horizon; fx_volume_climax_reversal found one-day shocks on majors continue and tick volume is not a capitulation signal; trend_follow found price-only trend flat before costs; jpy_cross_losscut_after_us_shock found no Tokyo-session continuation after a US equity shock and a Tokyo-morning bounce instead, and its mirror long was banned as a snooped flip. None of those had a positioning measure; this test conditions on the retail book itself, has no equity shock, holds for days rather than one session, and carries a control arm (the same decline without accumulation) that isolates the retail-book effect from the plain decline. Costs: AUDJPY spread 0.9 pips, 0.2 pips of slippage each side, 7 dollars commission (about 0.9 pips), about 2.2 pips against a 1.0 x ATR20 stop of 58 to 127 pips (median 81), so about 0.03 R; swap on a short AUDJPY is about -0.4 pips a day in the design years, negligible. Counts (design 2010-2017, from the TFX and price tables, no outcomes): AUDJPY about 460 days with a five-day decline of at least 1 x ATR20 (about 270 at 1.5 x); heavy10 covers about 45 percent of them, heavy15 about 30 percent, light (growth <= 2 percent) about 15 percent; with one position at a time and 2 to 4 weekday holds about 80 to 120 heavy trades and 30 to 45 light trades on AUDJPY; USDJPY, NZDJPY and GBPJPY similar; EURJPY's retail book was not structurally long in 2010-2017 (median five-day open-interest change on declines -2 percent), so it is the natural placebo market and is expected to show the weakest heavy-arm result. Expected: heavy arms +0.08 R, trade-level design t about 1.0 on about 100 trades, light arm at or below zero; P(design t >= 2 | effect real at +0.08 R) about 0.15, so an inconclusive negative is the most likely outcome, but this is the first measurement of the retail-flow dimension and its control fixes the baseline for every later TFX idea. Speed: about 1.1 trades a month per market, about 4 a month pooled over the four structurally long crosses (correlated), score 0.08 x 2 = 0.16: an edge-recording test that reaches the challenge speed bar only at +0.35 R. Human execution: one check of the TFX daily statistics after midnight UTC, one market order at the Tokyo open on the qualifying days, a 1 x ATR20 stop and a time exit two or four weekdays later at 20:30 UTC; a few orders a month. Most likely failure: the accumulation is the normal state of the book, so the heavy arms are the decline itself and the light arm is too small to tell; or the fragility shows only in the two or three crash episodes of the design years and dies in the breadth test.

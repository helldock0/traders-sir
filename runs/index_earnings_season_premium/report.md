# Aggregate earnings-announcement premium: hold the Nasdaq 100 CFD long Monday-to-Friday only in the weeks when most S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 companies report (weeks 2 to 6 after each quarter-end), with every other week of the year as the control arm; replication on the S&P 500 and Nikkei 225 CFDs  (`index_earnings_season_premium`)

Family: announcement_risk_premium_index | verdict: **kill:stage_a** | failing gates: GA_stage_a_floor | 2.0s

## Rationale
Mechanism: a risk premium, not a flow. Earnings news is systematic information; investors who hold the index through the weeks when most companies report bear concentrated cash-flow and discount-rate news and may be paid for it on average, as Savor and Wilson (2013) show for scheduled macro-announcement days (where the market's excess return is concentrated) and as Savor and Wilson (2016) and Barber, De George, Lehavy and Trueman (2013) show at the firm level for announcers against non-announcers. The firm-level spread cancels in a cap-weighted index, so neither paper gives an index-level size; this test asks whether any index-level excess exists in reporting weeks, and the honest prior is small: 0 to +0.1 percent a week (0 to +0.08 R at a 1.0 x ATR20 stop). Reporting is concentrated by the calendar: large US companies report from about the second week after a quarter-end for four to five weeks; Tokyo companies with a March year-end about two weeks later. The programme has never conditioned an index hold on the reporting calendar; its index work tested calendar flows (turn-of-month, option expiry, pension rebalancing), overnight and pre-holiday drift, intraday momentum, FOMC drift, dip-buying and vol timing. The charter's warning that long-only index tests pass by luck is met inside the design: the control arm is long in every non-season week of the same years, so drift, costs and financing are identical in both arms and only the reporting-week excess is scored, paired by calendar quarter. Confound stated up front: every season window contains the first-month-of-quarter FOMC meeting (about 20-22 percent of season weeks hold a decision against about 12 percent of control weeks) and prior_fomc_cycle found a pre-FOMC drift since 2016, so the difference is also reported with FOMC weeks dropped from both arms and the hypothesis is scrapped if that difference is not positive in design. Design: H1 bars on the Nasdaq 100 CFD (USATECHIDXUSD) from 2013 (2011 has six days of data; 2012 bars carry no spread), one trade per week, long at the open of the 15:00 UTC Monday bar, exit at the open of the 20:00 UTC Friday bar (the last bar of the Friday session in US daylight time), stop 1.0 or 2.0 x ATR20, no target, with a causal fallback exit over the weekend gap. Season weeks are fixed from the calendar before any data is seen (Monday start_off = 10 or 17 days after the quarter start, n_weeks = 4 or 5), bracketing the reporting calendar without reading it from the data. Counts (design 2013-2017, from the calendar and the bar census, no outcomes): about 70-90 season weeks and 140-170 control weeks per cell; check 2018-2023 similar; USA500IDXUSD from 2013, JPNIDXJPY from 2014 (about 60-70 design season weeks) with the 14-day shift. Costs (engine index costs: raw spread, 1 point of slippage per fill, financing per night with Wednesday tripled, so 6 nights for a Monday-to-Friday hold): about 0.15-0.25 percent of the index per week in 2013-2014, less later; a 1.0 x ATR20 stop is about 1.5-2 percent of the index, so about 0.10-0.13 R at sl 1.0 and 0.05-0.07 R at sl 2.0. Expected: the control near 0 to +0.05 R in design (the earlier non-opex weekly Nasdaq long, index_flows C1, made -0.019 R in design and +0.067 R in check); the season arm 0 to +0.08 R above it. Power is low: with about 85 season and 165 control weeks the standard error of the difference is about 0.14 R, so P(design paired-D t >= 1 | effect real at +0.05 to +0.08 R) is about 0.25-0.35 and about 0.16 if there is no effect; a kill is weak evidence, a pass is modest evidence, and the pass bar (design t >= 2) is reachable only if the effect is much larger than the prior. The most likely outcome is an inconclusive negative, which still records that the reporting calendar carries no weekly-hold excess large enough to trade on a CFD. Human execution: one order a week for about 16-20 weeks a year, Monday morning New York, Friday afternoon exit; executable on a prop account that allows index CFDs.

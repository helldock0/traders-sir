# Liquidity-withdrawal reversal: fade a large one-hour move only when the quoted spread widened during it (quotes withdrawn), with a no-spread-condition control and a scheduled-news control at stage A, on USDJPY first  (`fx_liquidity_withdrawal_reversal`)

Family: liquidity_provision_spread_state | verdict: **kill:stage_a** | failing gates: GA_stage_a_floor | 9.7s

## Rationale
Mechanism (Nagel 2012, 'Evaporating liquidity'; Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2009; Mancini, Ranaldo and Wrampelmeyer 2013 for FX): short-term price reversals are the compensation liquidity providers earn for absorbing order imbalances, and that compensation is large only when liquidity supply is scarce. In FX the scarcity is observable: when dealers and ECN liquidity providers pull or widen their quotes, the top-of-book spread widens against its normal level at that time of day. A large move that happens while quotes are withdrawn is, in part, a price concession paid by an impatient liquidity demander (a stop cascade, a forced hedger, a margin-driven seller) to the few providers still quoting; once quoting resumes, the concession is competed away and the price retraces. The same-sized move made with normal, tight quotes is information being priced by a deep book and should not retrace; this is what every prior fade in this programme measured, and they all lost (session reversal, Asian-extreme fade, quiet-hours Bollinger fade -0.062 R, sweep fades, one-day shock fade -0.10 R on EURUSD; and after 3292 USDJPY box stop-outs the price kept going the stop way, +0.08 R at 30 minutes and +0.11 R at one hour, gj-strat after_sl). Those tests conditioned on clock, level or size, never on the state of liquidity supply, so their negatives are consistent with this hypothesis rather than against it, and any edge here must come from the spread condition alone; the tick-volume test (fx_volume_climax_reversal) tried to measure liquidity demand with a quantity that does not measure it, and its lesson forbids volume, not spread. The quoted spread is the one data dimension in this store that no prior test has used as a signal input (its only earlier appearance is the entry spread as one feature inside the box_meta logistic filter). What the arm measures in practice (the Critic's counts, design years, no outcomes): the typical qualifying episode is a one-hour mid-price move of at least 0.25 x ATR20 (median 40 pips on USDJPY) during which the bar's quoted-spread proxy widens from a slot median of about 0.5 pips to about 1.3 pips (median ratio 2.25), outside the scheduled-announcement blocks; in 70 percent of flagged bars an M1 open or close quote inside the bar was at least twice the norm, so the proxy mostly reflects real quotes. It is an operational definition of mild, frequent quote withdrawal in fast markets, not of dealers leaving the market; the famous episodes are mostly not in the tested arm (2011-03-17 and 2013-05-23 contribute nothing, 2010-05-06 one bar at 23:30 UTC, 2015-08-24 four episodes, 2016-06-24 three). Design: bars M15 on USDJPY (0.6 pip median spread; the classic liquidity-gap currency). The state is the bar's spread proxy relative to the median of the same 15-minute slot over its previous 20 occurrences, so the daily spread profile cancels out; the rollover window [20:45, 22:15) UTC is always excluded because its asymmetric widening distorts the mid price. Two controls sit in the stage A grid: spread_ratio_min = 0 (no spread condition; the plain intraday shock fade, which the mechanism and the prior evidence say is about zero or negative) and exclude_news = false (keeps moves inside the scheduled-release blocks; these are information-driven and the mechanism predicts no reversal there, which the NFP fade result of -0.12 R already suggests). The hypothesis arm is spread_ratio_min 2 with exclude_news true at move_atr_min 0.25; the controls run only at stage A, and stages B, C, the walk-forward and any holdout run the hypothesis arm alone, because the pipeline ranks cells by design t and a 2,000-trade control would otherwise outrank a 135-trade hypothesis cell. Sample: USDJPY has 160 design episodes on 123 distinct trading days at move_atr_min 0.25 (83 at 0.40, a stage A surface point only; the earlier 135-on-105 count came from a float comparison that dropped exact-tie bars and was corrected at code review before any run); a few dates hold many of them (2015-08-24/25, the 2016 US election night, Brexit, a BoJ day), so t is also reported date-clustered. Stage B uses each market's own release blocks as well as the US ones, which leaves EURUSD 64, GBPUSD 35, AUDUSD 24, EURJPY 41, GBPJPY 37, AUDJPY 17 and XAUUSD 82 design episodes: a thin replication of about 300 episodes over seven markets, read pooled and date-clustered rather than market by market. Costs: the spread at the fill averages 0.90 pips at entry_delay_bars 0 and 0.83 at delay 1, so the round trip is about 1.8-1.9 pips (half the spread each way, 0.4 pip of slippage, about 0.7 pip of commission), about 0.05 R on the median 40-pip move, about 0.08 R at the p10 move and about 0.10 R at 2x costs; 0.04-0.08 R on the stage B markets (GBPJPY and AUDJPY fills average 5.9 and 4.5 pips after a flag). The entry_delay_bars = 1 arm is a latency and timing check (how fast the reversion happens and what 15 minutes of execution delay costs), not a cheaper fill. Expected: a net +0.08 R needs a gross of about +0.13 R, a mean retracement of about 13 percent of the move within two hours; with 160 episodes and sd 0.6-0.7 R the expected design t is 1.3-1.6 trade-level and lower date-clustered, so stage A has only about a 60 percent chance of passing even if the effect is real: a kill is weak evidence and a pass is strong evidence. Live execution caveat, stated up front: the trigger is defined on Dukascopy's quote feed, a prop-firm feed widens on its own schedule, so live signals must be computed from a Dukascopy feed and executed on the prop account; the setup fires about 17 times a year at unpredictable hours (44 percent between 23:00 and 03:00 UTC), so it needs an EA rather than a screen; and the executor's intrabar stop fill at the level plus 0.2 pip is optimistic in exactly these thin markets (expected bias small, about 0.01-0.02 R with a 40-pip stop, but with a fat tail), which the stop-exit gap report in stage C is there to show.

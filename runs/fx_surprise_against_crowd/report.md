# Positioning-amplified surprise continuation: trade in the direction of a high-impact scheduled data surprise 15 minutes after the release, only when CFTC leveraged funds are crowded on the other side of that surprise, holding to the New York afternoon of the release day or of the next weekday; with surprise-agrees-with-the-crowd and uncrowded control arms that trade the same surprises  (`fx_surprise_against_crowd`)

Family: event_positioning_unwind_fx | verdict: **kill:stage_a** | failing gates: GA_stage_a_floor | 5.2s

## Rationale
Mechanism: a forced unwind, not an information drift. Who trades: leveraged funds (the CFTC Traders in Financial Futures 'leveraged funds' group: hedge funds and CTAs) holding a crowded directional book in a currency future, measured as the net position as a share of open interest ranked against its own last 52 weekly readings, known from the Friday 15:30 New York release. Why they must: a scheduled high-impact release that surprises against that book moves the price against them within the first minutes (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Vega 2003: the conditional mean reaction is complete in about five minutes, which is why this test does not trade the jump itself); risk limits, stop-losses, VaR and end-of-day marks then force position cuts, and large funds execute those cuts over hours (order splitting, waiting for liquidity), so forced flow keeps arriving after the first reaction. Evans and Lyons (2005, 2008) show that most of the exchange-rate response to macro news is transmitted through induced order flow spread over hours and days rather than through the instantaneous repricing, and Brunnermeier, Nagel and Pedersen (2008) show that crowded speculator positions in the CFTC data unwind abruptly and produce the negative skew of currency returns. Who is on the other side: dealers who warehouse the flow intraday and slower real money. Why it shows up in price only in that state: when positioning is neutral, or when the surprise agrees with the crowd, there is no forced seller or buyer after the first minutes, so the post-15-minute drift should be near zero; the hypothesis is the asymmetry between the arms, not the sign of post-release drift as such. What has been measured before: edge_hunt tested NFP follow and fade on USDJPY, EURUSD and gold (92 events, 1 to 3 hour holds, direction read from the first move, no surprise size and no positioning) and found nothing; cot_positioning tested eight standalone positioning rules (crowding fades, hedging pressure, one-week flow follow and reversal) and found nothing after weekly costs; cb_drift and fomc_cycle measured decision-day drift unconditionally; the news-inclusive arm of fx_liquidity_withdrawal_reversal measured fades of one-hour price moves on USDJPY with a two-hour exit, not surprise-signed trades. None conditioned a post-release trade on positioning, and the external README names positioning as a conditioning state. Costs: EURUSD median spread 0.35 pips, perhaps 0.6 to 1.0 pips in the quarter hour after a release; with 0.2 pips of slippage on each side and the 7 dollar commission (about 0.7 pips) the round trip is about 1.5 to 2.5 pips against a 1.0 x ATR20 stop of 50 to 105 pips (median 75), so about 0.02 to 0.03 R; the other five markets cost 0.03 to 0.05 R. Counts from the calendar and COT tables only (design 2010-2017, EURUSD, no price outcomes): qualifying releases with |dz| >= 1.0 split against / with / neutral as 101 / 113 / 235 at crowd_q 0.8 and 69 / 77 / 303 at 0.9; with |dz| >= 1.5: 49 / 51 / 127 and 32 / 33 / 162; GBPUSD 133 / 149 / 321, AUDUSD 137 / 132 / 260, USDJPY 76 / 81 / 240, USDCAD 136 / 126 / 318 at z 1.0 and q 0.8; about 414 release days a year-set on EURUSD, 1.25 qualifying releases per release day. Expected: the against arm +0.12 R (about 9 pips of drift on a 75-pip stop) with trade-level design t about 1.3 on about 100 trades at crowd_q 0.8 and z_min 1.0, the with arm and the neutral arm within +-0.05 R of zero after costs; P(design t >= 2 | effect real at +0.12 R) about 0.3, so a kill is weak evidence and a pass is strong evidence. Speed: about 1 trade a month per market in the against arm, about 5 a month pooled over the six markets but clustered on US release days, so the speed score at expectation is 0.12 x sqrt(5) = 0.27: this is an edge-recording test, it reaches the challenge speed bar only if the against arm earns +0.3 R or more, and even a below-bar positive result is useful as a timing filter on the parked USDJPY legs around releases. Human execution: wait for the release, read actual against consensus on the calendar, check the crowd state from the Friday COT report, place one market order 15 minutes after the release with a 1 x ATR20 stop, exit 20:30 UTC the same day or the next weekday; two to six orders a month across the pairs. Most likely failure: the unwind is finished inside the first 15 minutes (ABDV) or the dealers lean against it so the drift is below 0.05 R, or crowdedness measured weekly is too stale to identify the book that is hurt on the day.
